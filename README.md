# HumanPhysiology
A day in the life of the human body: a fun, standalone educational web app about human physiology, with a deep-dive page for each organ.

Open `index.html` in any modern browser. No build step and no dependencies (Google Fonts are optional).

- **Scrub through 24 hours** (or press play): sleep, breakfast, lunch, an evening run, dinner.
  The see-through body changes as you go: the heart beats, the lungs breathe, blood flows, food travels down, sweat drips and Zzz float.
- **Heart–lung circulation**: red blood cells travel two circuits in series: heart → lungs → heart → an organ → heart. They turn red in the lungs and give up oxygen in each organ, with the amount depending on the organ and the activity. Organs are chosen in proportion to their share of the cardiac output, so in the run most blood goes to the legs and comes back dark. Flow surges with each heartbeat and venous return speeds up with each in-breath. The heart fills and empties in time with the ECG, and breaths have an active in-breath and a passive out-breath.
- **Six body systems you can switch on and off**: circulatory, nervous, endocrine, respiratory, digestive and urinary. Show any combination; one at a time is *live* (animated) and the others are drawn faded and still.
  The nervous system fires action potentials driven by the hour's physiology: motor and sympathetic traffic in the run, vagal volleys on each out-breath at rest, gut sensory and enteric traffic after meals, and slow waves in the sleeping brain.
  In the endocrine system, the glands secrete hormones (melatonin, growth hormone, thyroid hormone, cortisol, adrenaline, insulin, glucagon and GLP-1) that ride the bloodstream through the heart and lungs to their target organs. Islet and gut hormones pass through the liver first.
  In the respiratory system, air moves in through the nose (the mouth in the run) and out again with the volume of each breath. Only the first part of each in-breath reaches the alveoli; the rest stays in the dead space and is breathed straight back out. The diaphragm descends as the phrenic nerves fire, and in the run the rib and belly muscles join in.
  In the digestive system, waves of contraction sweep the stomach about three times a minute after a meal and squirt chyme into the duodenum, the gallbladder squeezes out bile and the pancreas sends its juice, chyme is mixed back and forth along the small intestine, and absorbed nutrients leave in the portal vein for the liver. The colon fills slowly and empties in mass movements after meals, most of all after breakfast. When fasting, the migrating motor complex sweeps the gut clean every ~100 minutes, and the run puts a brake on it all.
  In the urinary system, filtrate leaves the glomeruli at the rate of the GFR and runs down a nephron in each kidney, and waves of muscle push urine down the ureters. Urine is pale by day and dark at night or in the run, when ADH is high. The bladder fills slowly and empties at six trips to the toilet through the day, and in the run the kidneys release renin.
  The live system has numbered fact pins on the body and a card in the side panel explaining it for the current part of the day, both at the chosen reading level.
- **Follow a red blood cell**: tap a cell on the body (or the button in the circulatory card) and the camera rides along with it through the right heart, lungs, left heart, an organ and back.
  A card shows where it is, its O₂ saturation, how much oxygen each organ takes, laps and how long a real lap takes right now. The side panel explains each stop at the chosen reading level.
- **Follow a nerve impulse in bullet time**: tap a nerve on the body (or the button in the nervous-system card) and ride one action potential along nine pathways: motor commands to the thigh and arm, the knee-jerk reflex, touch from the foot to the sensory cortex, the "I'm full" signal from the stomach, vagus and sympathetic nerves to the heart, the sympathetic nerve to the adrenal gland and the vagus to the stomach.
  Time slows thousands of times, so the heart, breathing, blood and clock almost stand still. A card shows the stop, the fibre type and its speed, real elapsed time in milliseconds and how much time is slowed. At each synapse transmitter puffs across the gap. On arrival the effect plays (the muscle contracts, the next beat comes late, adrenaline is released) and time catches up. The side panel explains each stop at the chosen reading level.
- **Follow a hormone cascade in time-lapse**: tap a gland, a kidney or a moving hormone (or the button in the endocrine card) and follow a hormone signal on its own clock.
  Seconds: adrenaline from the adrenal gland speeds up the heart. Minutes: GLP-1 → insulin after breakfast (liver first, then GLUT4 in muscle), and glucagon → liver glucose → running legs. Hours: the stress axis CRH → ACTH → cortisol with negative feedback, and dusk → body clock → neck ganglion → pineal melatonin. Days and weeks: TRH → TSH → thyroxine → T3 acting on genes, and EPO from an oxygen-starved kidney → bone marrow → new red cells.
  Time speeds up from about ×2 to ×100,000 (and slows down again for the quick nerve steps). The day clock really moves, so you can watch glucose fall as insulin arrives or night fall as melatonin rises. A card shows the stop, the messenger, real elapsed time, the time-lapse factor and the day. Replay goes back to the start hour. The side panel explains each stop at the chosen reading level.
- **Follow a breath, in step with your breathing**: tap the nose, the windpipe, the lungs, the diaphragm or a moving air dot (or the button in the respiratory card) and follow one of four journeys: oxygen from the air to a mitochondrion in the thigh, where it becomes water; carbon dioxide from the muscle, hidden in the blood as bicarbonate, out through the nose; extra CO₂ → brainstem chemoreceptors → phrenic nerve → a deeper breath; and a speck of dust caught in mucus and swept up the windpipe by cilia.
  Air legs run in real time and are locked to the body's own breathing, so the molecule waits for the next in-breath or out-breath and goes faster in the run. The quick leaps across membranes are slowed down, and the blood rides and the 20-minute mucus escalator are sped up. A card shows the stop, the molecule's partial pressure falling down the oxygen cascade (≈160 → 100 → 40 → a few mmHg) or rising with CO₂, and the time factor. The side panel explains each stop at the chosen reading level.
- **Follow a bite, in time-lapse**: tap the mouth, the stomach, the gallbladder, the gut or a moving dot (or the button in the digestive card) and follow one nutrient from a bite of food: starch → glucose (saliva and pancreatic amylase, the brush border, SGLT1, the portal vein, the liver, GLUT4 in a thigh muscle); fat → chylomicron (bile, lipase, micelles, the lacteal and thoracic duct, which skip the liver, then lipoprotein lipase); protein → amino acids (acid and pepsin, trypsin, PepT1, the liver, new muscle protein); and fibre → gut bacteria → out (fermented into short-chain fatty acids in the colon and leaving a day or so later).
  The ride starts with a bite of the meal that is on, or jumps to the next meal. Time speeds up from seconds in the mouth to hours in the stomach and small intestine and a day or more in the colon, and the day clock really moves, so you can watch blood sugar rise after the meal and night fall while the fibre is still on its way. A card shows the stop, the molecule, real time elapsed and the time-lapse factor. The side panel explains each stop at the chosen reading level.
- **Follow a drop, in time-lapse**: tap a kidney, the bladder or a moving drop (or the button in the urinary card) and follow one of four journeys through the kidney: a sip of water (absorbed by the gut, filtered, one of the few molecules not taken back, then ADH and aquaporin-2 in the collecting duct decide); protein waste as urea (made in the liver, recycled in the inner medulla to help save water, then passed); glucose (filtered and taken straight back by SGLT2); and renin → angiotensin II (made by ACE in the lungs) → aldosterone from the adrenal gland → ENaC, so the kidney keeps salt.
  Time speeds up from seconds in the blood to minutes in the nephron and hours in the bladder, where the molecule waits for the body's next trip to the toilet, and the day clock really moves. A card shows the stop, the molecule, real time elapsed, the time-lapse and when the next pee will be. The side panel explains each stop at the chosen reading level.
- **Real sleep stages**: the night runs through five 90-minute cycles of light (N1, N2), deep (N3) and REM sleep, shown as a hypnogram above the timeline.
  Deep sleep brings slow brain waves, the calmest heart and regular breathing. N2 has sleep spindles. REM brings darting eyes, paralysed limbs, snoring, irregular breathing and heart-rate surges.
- **Live vitals**: heart rate with a scrolling ECG (including the breath-by-breath speeding and slowing of respiratory sinus arrhythmia) and stroke volume, breathing rate and tidal volume, blood glucose (with a 24 h trace), core temperature, blood pressure, cardiac output, arterial → venous blood O₂, and insulin, glucagon, cortisol, melatonin and adrenaline.
- **Thirteen organs to explore**: brain, heart, lungs, stomach & gut, liver, pancreas, kidneys, muscles, skin, bones, eyes, ears and throat.
  Each has a close-up illustration and text for each phase of the day (Sleep, Rest, Meal, Exercise).
  The close-ups are live simulations driven by the same heartbeat, breathing and physiology as the body:
  synapses fire and the brain is rinsed in sleep, heart cells contract with the ECG, red cells load O₂ in the alveoli,
  the stomach churns and villi absorb, blood seeps through liver lobules, islets release insulin and glucagon,
  filtrate runs through the nephron, sarcomeres slide with each running stride,
  skin cells climb and flake off while sweat beads and heat escapes in the run, bone marrow releases new red cells
  while osteoclasts and osteoblasts trade calcium and osteocytes flash with every footstep,
  the pupil follows the light while rods, cones and the body-clock cells of the retina fire and the eyes dart in REM sleep,
  hair cells sway on the cochlea's travelling wave and balance fluid sloshes with each stride,
  and the throat swallows, snores and breathes while cilia sweep mucus up the windpipe.
- **Organ deep dives**: each organ can get its own standalone page in `organs/`, opened from its card on the body ("Explore the heart in depth") and linking back to it at the same reading level and time of day.
  Each page covers the organ's anatomy, physiology and what it means to every body system (circulatory, nervous, endocrine, respiratory, digestive, urinary, immune and lymphatic, muscles and skeleton, skin and reproductive), and six of those cards link back to the body with that system animated.
  - **The heart** (`organs/heart.html`): a frontal section with chambers, valves, papillary muscles and wall layers, where the valves open and close and blood flows beat by beat. A live Wiggers diagram and pressure–volume loop come from a time-varying elastance model, with sliders for preload, afterload and contractility. The coronary arteries (LAD, diagonals, circumflex, marginals, RCA, PDA and the cardiac veins) sit on a beating 3D heart you can turn by dragging, with front, side and underside views; blood dots flow through them with the simulated coronary flow (mostly between beats on the left), and picking a vessel turns the heart to face it. They can be set right-, co- or left-dominant; block any segment to see the clot, the stalled vessels downstream, the starved wall darken and bulge instead of squeezing, a close-up of the plaque and clot inside the artery, its territory on the 17-segment bull's-eye, the ST changes on a 12-lead ECG, slower coronary flow and a weaker pump. The conduction system (SA node, atrial pathways, Bachmann's bundle, AV node, bundle of His, bundle branches, Purkinje fibres) shows the electrical wave in slow motion, with pacemaker and muscle action potentials over the ECG. Pick a rhythm: AV blocks (first degree, Wenckebach, Mobitz II, complete), bundle branch blocks, atrial fibrillation or sinus arrest. One simulated heart drives every section, so a dropped beat is also a missing pulse. A bar under the header lists every change you have made (a blocked artery, a rhythm, the autonomic balance, the load sliders, coronary dominance) with the other sections it reaches. Each of those sections explains the knock-on effect in a “Because of …” note, for example a proximal RCA block slowing the SA node in the conduction section and shrinking the pressure–volume loop in the physiology section. Undo one change or reset them all, and copy a link that opens the page with the same changes.
  - **The lungs** (`organs/lungs.html`): the airway tree, lobes, fissures, pleura, diaphragm, ribs and pulmonary vessels breathing in time, with close-ups of an acinus (alveoli, type I and II cells, a macrophage, capillaries) and of a bronchial wall (cartilage, smooth muscle, beating cilia, goblet cells, mucus). Live traces of pleural and alveolar pressure, airflow and volume come from a compliance–resistance model, with sliders for both. Take a spirometry test to see lung volumes, FEV₁, FVC and the flow–volume loop. Follow one red cell along a capillary as it loads O₂, change the altitude to see the O₂ cascade, shift the O₂–haemoglobin curve (temperature, 2,3-BPG, pH, fetal haemoglobin) and see V/Q from apex to base. The control circuit (brainstem, central chemoreceptors, carotid and aortic bodies, stretch receptors, phrenic nerve) runs a chemoreflex: hold your breath to the break point, hyperventilate, breathe 5% CO₂ or climb to Everest and watch PaO₂, PaCO₂ and ventilation. Pick a condition (asthma, COPD, fibrosis, pneumonia or pulmonary embolism) and it carries through every section. As on the heart page, a bar under the header lists what you have changed (the condition, compliance and resistance, altitude, the O₂-curve shifts, 5% CO₂, a breath-hold or hyperventilation) with the sections each change reaches, and “Because of …” notes explain the knock-on effects, e.g. altitude driving the chemoreflex in the control section and bigger breaths in the breathing section. Undo one change or reset them all, and copy a link that opens the page with the same changes.
  - **The brain** (`organs/brain.html`): the brain from the side (lobes, motor and sensory strips, Broca’s, Wernicke’s, hearing and vision areas) or cut down the middle (corpus callosum, thalamus, hypothalamus, pituitary, pineal, brainstem, cerebellum, ventricles, meninges and skull), with cerebrospinal fluid flowing from the ventricles to the veins and each region glowing with how busy it is right now. Close-ups show an impulse jumping along a myelinated axon and a synapse releasing transmitter onto its receptors while an astrocyte mops up. A Hodgkin–Huxley membrane fires live, with Na⁺ and K⁺ channels opening over the voltage trace: change the stimulus, block either channel or raise the potassium outside until the neuron falls silent. Race an impulse to the toe along a myelinated and an unmyelinated axon, and strip the myelin as in MS. A sleep-lab recording (EEG, eye movements, chin muscle) follows the same five sleep cycles as the body, with alpha, spindles, K-complexes, delta and REM bursts labelled as they happen; scrub or time-lapse through the night on the hypnogram and the two-process chart (adenosine and the body clock), and add a 4 pm coffee or an all-nighter. Blood flows round the circle of Willis: block an artery to see its territory starve and the deficits it causes (or block a carotid and watch the circle reroute), and move blood pressure and CO₂ along the autoregulation curves. A bar under the header lists what you have changed (neuron and axon settings, the coffee and the all-nighter, a blocked artery, blood pressure and CO₂) with the sections each change reaches. A blocked artery darkens its territory in the anatomy figure, blood flow too low for the cortex quietens it, and an all-nighter keeps the brain awake through the night (activity, EEG and blood flow), each explained by a “Because of …” note. Undo one change or reset them all, and copy a link that opens the page with the same changes.
  - **The kidneys** (`organs/kidneys.html`): a kidney cut in half (capsule, cortex, pyramids, columns, papillae, calyces, pelvis, ureter, the arterial tree and the adrenal gland), with urine dripping from the papillae, coloured by how concentrated it is, and blood flowing in and out. Beside it a live nephron runs from the glomerulus to the collecting duct: filtrate enters at the rate of the GFR, most of it is taken back along the way, and its colour shows it concentrating down the loop and diluting on the way up, over a medulla shaded by its own gradient. In the filtration section, Starling forces are integrated along a glomerular capillary between its two arterioles. Sliders for blood pressure, afferent and efferent tone, filter area and Bowman’s pressure change it, autoregulation (myogenic and tubuloglomerular feedback) and angiotensin II on the efferent arteriole act on it, and an ACE inhibitor can be added. An autoregulation curve, a glucose titration curve with an SGLT2 inhibitor and a table of clearances go with it. The concentrating section has a countercurrent multiplier you can pump and flow step by step (or start from 300 and watch the gradient build), with a loop diuretic and urea recycling, and a time-lapse of water balance (one second is ten minutes) with ADH, thirst and the collecting duct: drink a litre, stop drinking, give desmopressin, or switch to central or nephrogenic diabetes insipidus or SIADH. The acid–base section shows ketoacidosis, diarrhoea, vomiting, under- and over-breathing on a Davenport diagram, from minutes to days of compensation, with the tubule cells moving H⁺, bicarbonate, ammonium, sodium and potassium as the kidney responds. As on the other pages, a changes bar lists what you have changed and the sections it reaches, with “Because of …” notes (a blocked ureter swelling the pelvis, a loop diuretic washing out the medulla and lowering potassium, chronic kidney disease shrinking the kidney and making the blood acidic). Scenarios include dehydration after a run, shock, a kidney stone, chronic kidney disease, a narrowed renal artery with an ACE inhibitor, diabetic ketoacidosis, SGLT2 inhibitors, loop diuretics, diabetes insipidus, SIADH, COPD and vomiting, all compared with a healthy kidney.
  - **The stomach & gut** (`organs/gut.html`): the tract from the oesophagus to the anus (stomach regions and sphincters, duodenum, jejunum, ileum, caecum and appendix, colon, rectum, with the liver, gallbladder and pancreas alongside), with the latest meal moving through it in time-lapse: the stomach swells and empties, dots pass through the small intestine as they are absorbed, and the remains reach the colon. Close-ups show the four layers of the wall (with the two nerve plexuses and the pacemaker cells flashing with each slow wave), a gastric gland in the body and one in the antrum (acid rising from the parietal cells, gastrin travelling from the G cells) and a villus with its crypts (cells migrating to the tip). One model runs the meal minute by minute: a grinding lag, then emptying limited by calories and slowed by fat; acid from the vagus, gastrin and histamine, braked by somatostatin, with the stomach pH set by the meal’s buffer; three small-intestinal stages that absorb as they go; and CCK, GLP-1, PYY, ghrelin and vagal firing that make you full or hungry. The motility section records slow waves and spikes in real time from the stomach, duodenum, ileum or colon, and shows a length of gut whose contents move by conservation of volume, so the same rings either grind against the pylorus, mix by segmentation, sweep through as the fasting housekeeper wave or push a mass movement after a meal. Pick water, milk, toast, a mixed meal or a fry-up and watch the meal clock. The digestion section has a parietal cell pumping acid (block the pumps with a PPI, or histamine with an H₂ blocker), the three phases of secretion over the meal, a gauge of acid against the lining’s defences (H. pylori or NSAIDs, and healing on acid suppression), an enterocyte taking up sugars, amino acids, fat, salt and water (with lactase, coeliac villi, cholera toxin and oral rehydration), and a day’s fluid ledger down to the stool’s Bristol type. The last section follows hormones and vagal impulses from the gut to the hypothalamus, charts hunger and fullness through the meal (add a GLP-1 drug), and shows the colon’s microbes, fed by fibre or cut down by antibiotics, with their short-chain fatty acids and the breath hydrogen of fermentation. As on the other pages, a changes bar lists what you have changed with the sections it reaches and “Because of …” notes (an ulcer drawn on the tract, flattened villi, slower emptying keeping the stomach full and making acid for longer, a positive breath test after milk without lactase). Scenarios include a duodenal ulcer, NSAIDs with a PPI, lactose intolerance, coeliac disease, cholera with and without oral rehydration, diabetic gastroparesis, opioids on a low-fibre diet, antibiotics, a GLP-1 drug, a run after a fry-up and a high-fibre diet, all compared with a healthy gut eating the same meal.
  - **Scenarios and a healthy reference** (all organ pages): the changes bar has a *Scenarios* menu of ready-made cases that set several controls at once and say what each shows, for example an inferior heart attack with complete heart block, heart failure, septic shock or an athlete’s heart; an asthma attack, COPD on a plane, fibrosis on a run or the summit of Everest; a middle cerebral artery stroke, fainting, a panic attack, multiple sclerosis or an all-nighter; a kidney stone, diabetes insipidus or SIADH; a duodenal ulcer, lactose intolerance, cholera or gastroparesis. Once anything is changed, an untouched healthy organ runs alongside for comparison: the heart’s pressure–volume loop and the lungs’ flow and volume traces show it dashed, readouts show ▲/▼ how far each value has moved from it, and the hero stats add the healthy value.
  - **Adding an organ**: copy `organs/_template.html` to `organs/<id>.html`, fill in its JSON blocks (text at four levels, parts, body-systems lens), draw its figures, and add it to `ORGAN_PAGES` in `index.html`. The shared engine sits between `ORGAN ENGINE` markers (and the Export menu between `EXPORT ENGINE` markers): edit them in the template and run `node organs/sync-engine.mjs` to copy them into every page (`--check` reports pages that are out of date). Give each figure an id, an `aria-label` and a short `data-export-name` so the Export menu can list it. A page can register its changes with `OrganPage.alter()` to get the changes bar, the “Because of …” notes, scenarios (`alter.scenarios` in its content) and shareable links, and use `OrganPage.delta()` to compare readouts with a healthy reference.
- **Export** (the body and every organ page): an *Export* button in the top bar opens a menu for the figure in focus (the one you last touched, or the one most on screen) or for the whole view.
  - **Pictures**: any vector figure as an **SVG** with the current theme's colours written in (the CSS variables don't travel otherwise), with a title and a description, or any figure, vector or canvas, as a **PNG** at 1×, 2× or 4×. Canvases are redrawn at that resolution rather than upscaled. The background is the page colour or transparent, and a caption strip (on by default) says what the figure is, where it comes from, the time of day, the phase, the reading level, your changes or scenario, and the link to that view.
  - **Animations**: one heartbeat, one breath, 3, 5 or 10 seconds, or (on the body) a whole journey from start to end, as a **WebM** video, an animated **GIF** or a ZIP of PNG frames. Frames are rendered one at a time while the page's clock is held, so the files come out smooth however slow the drawing, and loops of a beat or a breath join up. They follow the page's speed setting. The video uses WebCodecs with a small built-in WebM muxer, falling back to `MediaRecorder` (MP4 on Safari) in browsers without it; the GIF has its own palette and LZW encoder. A progress bar shows the recording, with a cancel button.
  - **The view**: *Save settings* writes a JSON file (`humanphysiology.view`, version 1) with the reading level, hour, speed, shown and live systems, the journey being followed and how far along it is, and on organ pages the selected parts, every control, the changes and the scenario. *Load settings…*, or dropping the file anywhere on the page, brings that view back, and a file saved on another page opens that page first. *Copy link* gives a shareable link (the body's link now carries `shown=`, `live=none` and `play=1` too). *Data (CSV)* exports the readouts and live traces with units in the headers: the vitals now and through the day on the body, the hero stats and every readout on the organ pages, plus the Wiggers recording (heart), the breathing traces (lungs), the action potential (brain) and the day's water balance (kidneys). *All figures (ZIP)* bundles every figure as SVG and PNG with the settings and data.
  - The menu is one shared engine, kept between `EXPORT ENGINE` markers in `organs/_template.html` and copied into every organ page and `index.html` by `node organs/sync-engine.mjs`. `node tests/export-check.mjs` (Playwright) opens every page, exports every figure as SVG and PNG, records a WebM, a GIF and PNG frames, round-trips a settings file with a scenario applied, and checks the CSV.
- **Four reading levels**: Middle school, High school, College and PhD / MD. A glossary links terms and shows their definitions as tooltips.

Values are typical for a healthy adult and simplified for teaching. This is not medical advice.

The structure follows the [Oak Year](../OakYear) template. See the architecture notes at the top of `index.html`.
